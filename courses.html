About the Course

This course develops the computational foundations of speech and language technology — how human language, in text and in speech, is represented, modelled, and processed by machines. Starting from the representation of raw text as sequences of code points, the course builds up through normalization, tokenization, and vector representations of documents, towards statistical and neural models of language and speech.

Course Outline

Module 1 — Representing Language Computationally
  1. The text representation problem
  2. Characters and Unicode
  3. Normalized text → subword tokens (BPE)
  4. Tokens → document vectors (Bag-of-Words, TF–IDF)
Module 2 — Predicting Language and Linguistic Labels
  1. N-gram language models (Ch. 3, Jurafsky & Martin)
